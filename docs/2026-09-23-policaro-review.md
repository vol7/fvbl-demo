# FVBL video v4: what changed, and what we'd like your call on

For Francesco, Gus and Fawaz, with the v4 review cut.

The review cut shows each voiceover line as a caption, because the voice is
recorded last, once the picture is locked. Shots we still have to record show
as grey slates with the shot number. Everything from the 2026-09-18 call is
in: the dealer's first registration up front, the happy path first, three
catches at the end, the roadmap, a voiceover throughout, the MTO sender and
Carfax as a source.

Before sending it to you we stress-tested the script with two simulated
reviews: an AI role-playing an MTO director who owns the UVIP process, and
another role-playing a CBSA director general on the federal auto-theft working
group. They are not real officials and not real feedback, but they raised
points worth deciding before this goes in front of the real ones. Where we
acted on them, it is listed below.

## Decisions we made, and would like you to confirm

**1. A 7-second cold open.** The video opens on the Range Rover whose VIN was
reported exported, flagged at an Ontario counter, then the title. On the
18th, the preference was the checks and balances first and the fraud last. The
happy path still comes first and nothing is explained before the dealer beat,
but the first image is now a red flag. Both simulated reviewers preferred it:
it tells a law-enforcement viewer why they are watching in the first seconds.
Dropping it is a one-line change. *Keep it or drop it?*

**2. Third-party, federal and US sources are described as conditional.** The
screens show insurers, Carfax, CBSA, Transport Canada and NMVTIS answering.
The voice says "with CBSA export records connected" and "with the right
agreements", from the opening on, not "we integrate CBSA", and a small corner
note on those shots reads "Concept. Third-party, federal and US sources are
illustrative. No data-sharing agreements are in place." If there is already an
agreement with Carfax or an insurer, tell us and we'll name it plainly. This was the
simulated CBSA reviewer's biggest objection: named as if they were a partner,
their first question would be when they signed. *Are you comfortable with
the caveat, or do you want the stronger "by integrating" wording?*

**3. Red means hold for review, and referrals go through MTO.** The portal now
says "Hold, do not issue" instead of "Package cannot be issued", and the
button is "Refer for investigation" instead of "Escalate to law enforcement".
A referral goes to MTO Investigations, who bring in the OPP (and CBSA for
cross-border cases) after review, and the clerk is told not to share the
reason with the customer. Before, it went straight to the RCMP and CBSA from
the counter. *Does that match how you, and Fawaz in particular, see the
referral path?*

**4. The hero leads with cloned VINs.** FVBL checks a car's identity at
registration rather than preventing theft, so the video opens on CARFAX
Canada's estimate that more than 372,000 vehicles in Canada may carry a cloned
VIN (2025 Year in Rear View), then says the same trick crosses the border and
that FVBL is built to catch it at the ServiceOntario counter. There is no
public non-Carfax count. On the 18th you asked us not to approach Carfax yet.
Citing a published figure isn't approaching them, but it does put their name
on the first frame. *Comfortable with that, or should we open on the problem
without a number?*

**5. The write-off catch leads with the cloned identity.** Ontario already
brands written-off vehicles, so the catch is a write-off that comes back under
someone else's VIN, not the write-off itself.

**6. The ServiceOntario footage carries a badge:** "Concept mock-up. Not an
Ontario government page." It is a copy of ontario.ca, and once the video is
forwarded nobody sees the context.

## Questions only you can answer

**7. Should the video end on an ask?** It currently ends on the roadmap map
and the title. Both simulated reviewers said they would want to know what is
being asked of them: a scoped pilot at a few ServiceOntario counters, and
which data from whom. We did not write one, because what to ask for, of whom
and when is your call. *Do you want an ask card, and what should it say?*

**8. What does a border officer get back?** The simulated CBSA reviewer said
the thing most likely to get federal enforcement to a second meeting is the
reverse direction: a flag raised at an Ontario counter reaching officers
checking containers at the port. A short beat before the map could show it.
*Worth adding, or too far for this version?*

**9. The owner's text.** The text comes from MTO with a link, as agreed. The
simulated MTO reviewer pointed out that government anti-phishing campaigns
tell people not to trust texts with links. We dropped "so they know it's
legit" from the voiceover. The real product will need an answer; for the
video, *is the link fine as it is?*

**10. A US-specific version.** For next week's US meeting, a variant could
open cold on the NMVTIS catch (the Lexus with an active Pennsylvania title)
instead of the Range Rover, and close on the US side of the map. Both are
swaps of existing shots once this cut is locked. *Would it help?*

## Things we checked

- The 372,000 figure is in CARFAX Canada's 2025 Year in Rear View
  (2025-11-25) and its 2025-09-10 most-cloned-vehicles release, up from
  141,260 potential clones the year before. It counts *potentially* cloned
  VINs, so the video says "may".
- The earlier theft figure, 19,319 vehicles stolen in Ontario in 2025 with a
  51% recovery rate, is in Équité Association's release of 2026-02-11. It is
  no longer in the video, but may be useful in the cover note.
- Separately, a CBSA release of 2026-08-05 says CBSA intercepted 1,590 stolen
  vehicles in railyards and ports in 2025. It is not in the video, but may be
  useful in the cover note.
- Public Safety Canada's National Action Plan on Combatting Auto Theft
  (fourth update, April 2026) says CBSA "continues to share available
  Import/Export Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) data with external
  partners, such as Équité and Carfax". The 2025-10-09 progress release
  described it as "implementing a strategy". Either way, that sharing is
  already in place. Carfax Canada already sells a VIN Fraud Check to dealers
  and Vehicle Monitoring to owners built on it. The answer to "why not just
  use Carfax" is where the check happens: a Carfax report is optional, pulled
  separately, and nothing obliges anyone to act on it. FVBL puts the flag in
  front of the clerk and holds the package. The bridge line says "today's
  counter checks" for this reason.
